1. Overview

OpenFront is a real-time strategy game about carving up a world map with up to a hundred other people at once. It reached over 200,000 players a day without games media ever writing about it.

200-250kPlayers daily
190,000Steam wishlists
100+Players per match
35+Languages

Figures as of 22 August 2026.

You start with a single territory and expand. So does everyone else. Alliances form mid-match because they have to, hold while they're useful, and break at the worst possible moment.

There is no base building and no unit micromanagement - the depth is diplomatic. What people remember from a match is almost never a battle. It's who betrayed whom, and when.

It runs in a browser tab. No download, no account, no waiting.

2. Background

OpenFront grew with no previews, no coverage, no press push, no publisher, no marketing budget, and no store page until 2026. It grew because people told other people there was a browser tab where a hundred strangers fight over a map and lie to each other about it.

A year ago it was a browser game with a small group of players and an open GitHub repository. Today it has a competitive scene, a Discord in the tens of thousands, and a contributor base that writes a substantial share of the codebase.

Gamescom 2026 is the first time OpenFront has been shown to press.

4. Recent Development

v33, released 3 August 2026, was the largest update in the game's history:

  • Doomsday Clock - a battle-royale gamemode where a closing zone drains troops and warships from anyone caught outside it, built to break late-game stalemates
  • Ranked 2v2 with its own ladder
  • 22 new maps, including the entire Solar System and the Las Vegas Strip
  • Impassable terrain - a new class of map design
  • Account usernames, custom tribe names, warship veterancy, and MIRVs that fly as real interceptable nukes

Following a migration from Canvas to WebGL rendering, v33 also delivered a substantial optimisation pass: core live memory down 45% on large maps, garbage collection churn down 75%, and late-game frame-time p95 down 65%. In practice, the point where a busy match begins to slow is much further away than it was.

6. Team

OpenFront is made by three people and a very large number of volunteers.

Evan Pellegrini

Founder & CEO

Wrote the first version of OpenFront and still writes the game today. Left a six-year engineering career at Google - latterly working on Gemini and other LLMs - to build a browser strategy game full time. Has final say on every change that goes into the codebase, including the ones from strangers.

Josh Harris

Technical Director

Keeps OpenFront standing up. Owns the backend behind a quarter of a million daily players and built the monitoring that warns him before it falls over. Splits his week between infrastructure and reviewing pull requests from contributors he has never met. Building the Steam client. Previously backend engineering at Monzo and multiplayer infrastructure at Gameye.

Lewis Malton

Managing Director / COO

Handles everything that isn't the game: partnerships, pricing, contracts, and the unglamorous operational work that keeps a company growing this fast from falling apart. Founded and runs OpenFront Masters, the game's competitive circuit - building an esports scene around a free browser game turned out to be harder than expected, and more interesting.

Dozens of external contributors ship code in every update. Players build the maps. All 35+ translations are volunteer work, including several languages nobody asked for. Every release publishes the full contributor list on the wiki, by name.
